# GRIOS v15 — Investment Research & Valuation Engine

## 1. Mission

Build an institutional-grade investment research and valuation engine capable of evaluating the economic quality, financial strength, competitive position, intrinsic value, and investment attractiveness of companies and financial assets.

The engine must transform fundamental, financial, industry, macroeconomic, and strategic information into evidence-based investment research.

Its purpose is to determine:

- What an asset is economically worth.
- What drives its fundamental value.
- Whether its current market price offers an attractive margin of safety.
- Whether its competitive advantages are sustainable.
- Whether its growth expectations are supported by financial evidence.
- Which companies and assets may benefit from structural megatrends.
- Whether a potential investment offers an acceptable risk-adjusted return.

**Core principle: A strong business is not automatically a good investment; valuation, expectations, timing, and risk determine investment attractiveness.**

## 2. Strategic Objectives

1. Analyze company financial statements and operating performance.
2. Evaluate business models and competitive advantages.
3. Estimate intrinsic value using appropriate valuation methodologies.
4. Identify overvaluation, undervaluation, and valuation uncertainty.
5. Analyze revenue growth, margins, profitability, cash flow, and capital efficiency.
6. Evaluate management quality and capital allocation.
7. Connect fundamental research with macroeconomic and industry conditions.
8. Identify beneficiaries of megatrends and strategic capital investment.
9. Compare market price with fundamental value.
10. Generate investment research reports with transparent assumptions and evidence.

## 6. Financial Quality and Accounting Integrity

Evaluate the quality and sustainability of reported earnings.

Monitor:

- Accruals
- Receivables growth relative to revenue
- Inventory accumulation
- Deferred revenue
- Capitalized expenses
- Stock-Based Compensation
- Non-recurring adjustments
- Related-party transactions
- Auditor qualifications
- Restatements
- Changes in accounting methodology

Flag material discrepancies between reported earnings and operating cash flow.

Distinguish legitimate accounting differences from potential financial-reporting concerns. Do not classify a company as fraudulent without sufficient evidence.

## 9. Growth and Financial Forecasting

Develop forward-looking financial scenarios.

Forecast:

- Revenue
- Gross Margin
- Operating Margin
- EBITDA
- EPS
- Free Cash Flow
- Capital Expenditure
- Working Capital
- Debt
- Share Count

Support three principal scenarios:

### Bull Case
Higher growth, favorable margins, stronger competitive positioning, or better industry conditions.

### Base Case
Most defensible assumptions supported by available evidence.

### Bear Case
Growth deterioration, margin pressure, competition, execution failure, or adverse macroeconomic conditions.

Every forecast must disclose:

- Assumptions
- Forecast horizon
- Historical reference period
- Key operating drivers
- Confidence level
- Sensitivity to major variables

Do not present analyst consensus or model estimates as guaranteed future results.

## 10. Valuation Methodologies

Support multiple valuation frameworks.

### A. Discounted Cash Flow (DCF)

Estimate intrinsic value through projected free cash flows and an appropriate discount rate.

Support:

- Unlevered Free Cash Flow
- Levered Free Cash Flow
- Weighted Average Cost of Capital
- Cost of Equity
- Terminal Growth Method
- Exit Multiple Method
- Sensitivity Analysis

All discount-rate and terminal-value assumptions must be documented.

### B. Comparable Company Analysis

Use relevant peer companies and appropriate valuation multiples.

Metrics:

- Price / Earnings
- Forward Price / Earnings
- EV / EBITDA
- EV / EBIT
- EV / Revenue
- Price / Book
- Price / Free Cash Flow
- Free Cash Flow Yield

Peers must be selected based on business model, growth, profitability, capital intensity, geography, and risk.

### C. Precedent Transaction Analysis

Analyze comparable mergers and acquisitions.

Evaluate:

- Transaction multiples
- Acquisition premiums
- Transaction date
- Industry conditions
- Deal structure
- Strategic rationale

Account for differences between control transactions and minority investments.

### D. Dividend Discount Model

For suitable dividend-paying companies, evaluate:

- Dividend Growth
- Payout Ratio
- Cost of Equity
- Dividend Sustainability
- Dividend Coverage

### E. Asset-Based Valuation

For appropriate businesses, evaluate:

- Net Asset Value
- Book Value
- Liquidation Value
- Replacement Cost
- Sum-of-the-Parts Valuation

### F. Sector-Specific Valuation

Support tailored approaches for:

- Banks and insurers
- REITs
- Commodity producers
- Mining companies
- Early-stage technology businesses
- Biotechnology companies
- Infrastructure operators

A single valuation methodology must not be imposed on every business model.

## 11. Intrinsic Value and Margin of Safety

For each suitable valuation, calculate:

- Estimated Intrinsic Value
- Current Market Price
- Upside to Intrinsic Value
- Downside to Intrinsic Value
- Margin of Safety
- Valuation Range
- Scenario-Weighted Value

Distinguish between:

- Market Price
- Estimated Fair Value
- Conservative Value
- Optimistic Value

Intrinsic value must be expressed as a range when assumptions create material uncertainty.

A discount to estimated intrinsic value does not automatically make an asset attractive if the business model, balance sheet, or cash flows are deteriorating.

## 13. Valuation Risk and Sensitivity Analysis

Test valuation sensitivity to:

- Revenue growth
- Operating margins
- Discount rates
- Terminal growth
- Capital expenditure
- Commodity prices
- Foreign-exchange rates
- Interest rates
- Debt costs
- Share dilution

Identify assumptions that contribute most to estimated value.

Run downside scenarios and evaluate the consequences of adverse changes.

Where the model is highly sensitive to small assumption changes, reduce valuation confidence.

## 14. Investment Quality Score

Create an Investment Quality Score from 0 to 100.

Proposed weighting:

| Component | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Business Quality & Competitive Advantage | 15% |
| Financial Strength & Balance Sheet | 15% |
| Earnings and Cash Flow Quality | 15% |
| Growth and Industry Positioning | 15% |
| Valuation Attractiveness | 20% |
| Management and Capital Allocation | 10% |
| Data Quality and Evidence | 10% |

Interpretation:

- 85–100: Exceptional fundamental profile
- 70–84: Strong fundamental profile
- 55–69: Mixed fundamental profile
- 40–54: Weak fundamental profile
- 0–39: Highly unfavorable fundamental profile

The Investment Quality Score must remain separate from the Valuation Score, Signal Score, and Portfolio Risk Score.

A high-quality company can be overvalued. A low valuation multiple can reflect deteriorating fundamentals.

## 19. Governance and Model Controls

- Financial figures must retain original reporting periods and currencies.
- Restated financial statements must be identifiable.
- Forecasts must remain distinguishable from reported financial results.
- Valuation assumptions must be transparent.
- Multiple valuation methods should be used when appropriate.
- Model outputs must be reproducible.
- Stale or incomplete data must reduce confidence.
- Analyst consensus must not be treated as verified company guidance.
- Valuation models must account for dilution and material liabilities.
- No valuation output guarantees future market price convergence.
- Research conclusions must preserve source references and audit history.

## 20. Final Operating Principle

GRIOS must determine not only which companies participate in the next major investment trend, but also which companies possess the economic characteristics, financial strength, competitive advantages, and valuation conditions necessary to capture that trend profitably.

**The objective is to distinguish a compelling narrative from a genuinely attractive investment.**
