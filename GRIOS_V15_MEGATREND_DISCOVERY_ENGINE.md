## 1. PURPOSE

The Megatrend Discovery Engine identifies structural economic, technological, industrial, geopolitical, and capital-allocation transformations before they become fully reflected in mainstream market narratives.

Its primary objective is to connect:

- Structural megatrends
- Technology adoption
- Demand expansion
- Supply-chain constraints
- Strategic materials
- Corporate capital expenditure
- Institutional capital allocation
- Public-market investment opportunities

The engine must distinguish between a genuinely developing structural trend and a temporary market narrative.

**Core principle:**

A megatrend is not automatically an investment opportunity. An investment opportunity requires evidence of economic value creation, identifiable beneficiaries, an investable instrument, a credible catalyst, and an acceptable risk profile.

## 3. MEGATREND LIFECYCLE MODEL

Every identified megatrend must be assigned a lifecycle stage.

### Stage 1 — Emergence

Characteristics:

- Early technological or industrial development
- Limited commercial adoption
- Initial research and development
- Early government or corporate funding
- Uncertain economic viability

### Stage 2 — Acceleration

Characteristics:

- Increasing commercial validation
- Growing corporate investment
- Rising procurement activity
- New supply agreements
- Increasing institutional attention
- Improving unit economics

### Stage 3 — Scaling

Characteristics:

- Expanding production capacity
- Significant capital expenditure
- Wider customer adoption
- Supply-chain expansion
- Increasing revenue contribution
- Infrastructure investment

### Stage 4 — Maturity

Characteristics:

- Established commercial markets
- Broad adoption
- More predictable demand
- Increased competition
- Greater sensitivity to valuation and margins

### Stage 5 — Saturation or Decline

Characteristics:

- Slowing incremental adoption
- Excess capacity
- Margin compression
- Technological substitution
- Structural demand deterioration

The engine must not assume that every trend progresses sequentially. Trends may stall, reverse, fragment geographically, or become obsolete.

## 5. EVIDENCE HIERARCHY

Every signal must carry an evidence classification.

**Tier 1 — Primary Evidence**

- Regulatory filings
- Audited financial statements
- Official government publications
- Official corporate disclosures
- Signed commercial agreements
- Verified procurement documents

**Tier 2 — High-Quality Independent Evidence**

- Established financial and industry publications
- Independent research institutions
- Verified industry data
- Credible supply-chain reporting

**Tier 3 — Supporting Evidence**

- Analyst research
- Industry commentary
- Expert interviews
- Secondary market research

**Tier 4 — Early Indicators**

- Industry rumors
- Unconfirmed reports
- Social-media discussions
- Speculative narratives

Tier 4 signals may initiate investigation but cannot independently establish an institutional investment thesis.

Each signal must include:

- Source
- Publication date
- Event date, if different
- Evidence tier
- Verification status
- Relevant entities
- Geographic exposure
- Confidence level
- Last verification timestamp

### Supply Analysis

An announced factory is not equivalent to actual production.

- Substitution risks

The engine should identify where economic value is captured, not merely where revenue growth occurs.

## 8. PUBLIC-MARKET BENEFICIARY MAPPING

The engine must identify potentially exposed public companies and investable instruments.

Each beneficiary must be classified as:

### Direct Exposure

The company directly produces the relevant technology, material, equipment, or service.

### Indirect Exposure

The company supplies infrastructure, components, materials, or supporting services.

### Strategic Exposure

The company may benefit from a structural shift through its assets, intellectual property, market position, or long-term contracts.

### Speculative Exposure

The relationship is plausible but insufficiently demonstrated.

For every identified company, evaluate:

- Ticker and exchange
- Business segment
- Revenue exposure
- Relevant customer concentration
- Contract visibility
- Capital expenditure requirements
- Margin sensitivity
- Competitive position
- Balance-sheet risk
- Valuation
- Geographic and regulatory exposure

Do not classify a company as a major beneficiary merely because its business description contains a relevant keyword.

## 9. ECONOMIC VALUE CAPTURE & BOTTLENECK ANALYSIS

The engine must identify which segments have the strongest potential to capture economic value.

Key questions:

- Where is supply structurally constrained?
- Which components have long replacement or construction lead times?
- Which suppliers possess pricing power?
- Which companies control scarce intellectual property?
- Which businesses benefit from high switching costs?
- Which segments require significant capital investment?
- Where could excess capacity destroy margins?

A bottleneck may create pricing power, but only if the supplier has sufficient commercial leverage and can convert demand into sustainable profitability.

## 10. MARKET EXPECTATIONS & VALUATION

A strong structural trend does not guarantee attractive equity returns.

The engine must assess:

- Current valuation
- Revenue growth expectations
- Earnings expectations
- Margin assumptions
- Market-implied growth
- Capital intensity
- Free cash flow conversion
- Competitive threats
- Potential multiple compression

The analysis must distinguish:

**Good Industry / Good Company / Good Investment**

These are three separate assessments.

A company may operate in an attractive industry while its stock is already priced for unrealistic growth.

## 11. GEOPOLITICAL & REGULATORY EXPOSURE

For every material megatrend, evaluate:

- US-China strategic competition
- Export controls
- Sanctions
- Trade restrictions
- Domestic industrial policies
- Critical-material dependencies
- Political stability
- National security restrictions
- Regulatory approvals
- Government funding dependency

Geopolitical risk must be analyzed as both a potential catalyst and a potential source of disruption.

## 12. SCENARIO ANALYSIS

Each major megatrend must include three scenarios.

### Bull Scenario

- Accelerating adoption
- Strong demand
- Favorable policy environment
- Improving economics
- Supply constraints benefiting established suppliers

### Base Scenario

- Gradual adoption
- Moderate capital expenditure
- Normalizing supply-demand conditions
- Mixed profitability across the value chain

### Bear Scenario

- Delayed commercialization
- Weak end-market demand
- Excess capacity
- Regulatory obstacles
- Technological substitution
- Capital expenditure reductions

Each scenario must specify:

- Trigger conditions
- Leading indicators
- Beneficiary segments
- Principal risks
- Evidence that would invalidate the scenario

## 13. MEGATREND SCORING MODEL

The Megatrend Discovery Score is a structural assessment, not a stock recommendation.

| Component | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Structural Demand Strength | 20% |
| Supply Constraints & Bottleneck Potential | 15% |
| Technology & Commercial Adoption | 15% |
| Corporate CapEx & Industrial Commitment | 15% |
| Strategic & Government Support | 10% |
| Verified Capital Allocation | 10% |
| Economic Value Capture Potential | 10% |
| Evidence Quality & Data Freshness | 5% |
| **Total** | **100%** |

### Score Classification

- 85–100: Exceptional Structural Trend
- 70–84: Strong Structural Trend
- 55–69: Developing Trend
- 40–54: Weak or Uncertain Trend
- 0–39: Insufficient Structural Evidence

### Scoring Rules

- Missing information must not automatically receive a neutral or positive score.
- Unsupported assumptions must be flagged.
- Conflicting evidence must reduce confidence.
- Scores must retain their component-level explanations.
- Confidence and structural score must remain separate.
- A high score does not imply a high probability of investment returns.

## 14. LEADING & LAGGING INDICATORS

### Leading Indicators

- New procurement agreements
- Announced corporate CapEx
- Permitting and construction milestones
- Technology commercialization
- Order-book expansion
- Equipment lead times
- Government funding commitments
- Capacity investment decisions

### Coincident Indicators

- Production volumes
- Actual equipment deliveries
- Capacity utilization
- Commercial deployment
- Revenue contribution

### Lagging Indicators

- Reported earnings
- Realized margins
- Historical stock performance
- Completed capacity expansions
- Reported market share

The engine must prioritize leading indicators while using coincident and lagging data to validate the thesis.

## 15. DISCONFIRMATION & EARLY WARNING SYSTEM

The engine must actively search for evidence against each megatrend thesis.

Examples:

- Demand forecasts repeatedly revised downward
- Project cancellations
- Delayed factory commissioning
- Declining order books
- Excess production capacity
- Falling utilization
- Customer concentration deterioration
- Technological substitution
- Regulatory restrictions
- Deteriorating unit economics
- Financing difficulties

A thesis must be downgraded when its supporting assumptions are materially invalidated.

The system must never retain a positive classification solely because a theme was previously popular.

## 18. OPERATIONAL WORKFLOW

The standard workflow is:

1. Discover emerging signals.
2. Register and classify evidence.
3. Validate source quality and freshness.
4. Identify structural demand drivers.
5. Analyze supply constraints.
6. Map the value chain.
7. Identify economic bottlenecks.
8. Track corporate and institutional capital allocation.
9. Identify listed beneficiaries.
10. Evaluate economic exposure and valuation.
11. Build bull, base, and bear scenarios.
12. Calculate the Megatrend Discovery Score.
13. Search for contradictory evidence.
14. Update the investment watchlist.
15. Publish relevant findings through the GRIOS reporting system.

## 19. GOVERNANCE & DATA INTEGRITY

The engine must preserve:

- Full source lineage
- Historical score changes
- Classification history
- Verification timestamps
- Original publication dates
- Data freshness indicators
- Confidence changes
- Human review status

The engine must distinguish:

- Confirmed facts
- Official announcements
- Independent reporting
- GRIOS analytical interpretation
- Model inference
- Speculative hypotheses

No company, institutional investor, government, or strategic transaction may be assigned an unsupported activity or exposure.

## 20. FINAL OPERATING PRINCIPLE

GRIOS must identify structural economic transformations before they become consensus market narratives.

However, early discovery alone is not sufficient.

The system must determine:

1. Whether the trend is real.
2. Whether it is accelerating.
3. Where capital is being deployed.
4. Which parts of the value chain capture economic value.
5. Which public companies have demonstrable exposure.
6. Whether the market has already priced in the opportunity.
7. What evidence would invalidate the investment thesis.

**GRIOS v15 Objective:**

Track structural change, verify capital allocation, identify economic bottlenecks, and detect investable opportunities before the broader market fully recognizes them.
