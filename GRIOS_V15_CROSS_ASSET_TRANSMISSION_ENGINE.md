## 1. PURPOSE

The Cross-Asset Transmission Engine identifies, measures, and monitors how macroeconomic, geopolitical, monetary, fiscal, and financial shocks propagate across global asset classes.

Its objective is to understand not only what happened in a specific market, but also:

- What caused the initial shock?
- Which economic or financial variable was affected first?
- Through which transmission channels did the shock propagate?
- Which asset classes are exposed?
- Which markets are confirming or contradicting the initial interpretation?
- Where could capital flows, repricing, or risk reallocation emerge?

The engine must connect macroeconomic developments to observable market behavior and actionable investment intelligence.

**Core principle:**

A market movement is not an isolated event. It is potentially one component of a broader transmission process involving liquidity, interest rates, currencies, credit, commodities, equities, and digital assets.

## 4. PRIMARY TRANSMISSION CHANNELS

The engine must identify the specific mechanism connecting an initial shock to market outcomes.

### 4.1 Interest Rate Channel

Track:

- Policy-rate expectations
- Overnight index swaps
- Short-term Treasury yields
- Long-term Treasury yields
- Yield curve structure
- Real yields
- Term premium

Potential transmission:

Policy repricing → Treasury yields → discount rates → equity valuations and financing costs.

### 4.2 US Dollar Channel

Track:

- DXY
- Major currency pairs
- Interest-rate differentials
- Global dollar funding conditions
- Foreign-exchange intervention
- Reserve allocation trends

Potential transmission:

Dollar appreciation → tighter dollar-denominated financial conditions → pressure on selected commodities, emerging-market currencies, and dollar-funded borrowers.

The relationship is conditional and must not be treated as universally stable.

### 4.3 Liquidity Channel

Track:

- Federal Reserve balance sheet
- Treasury General Account
- Reverse repo balances
- Bank reserves
- Repo market conditions
- Funding spreads
- Credit availability
- Financial conditions indices

Potential transmission:

Liquidity deterioration → tighter funding conditions → reduced risk capacity → potential pressure on leveraged assets.

### 4.4 Credit Channel

Track:

- Investment-grade spreads
- High-yield spreads
- Credit-default swap indicators
- Corporate refinancing conditions
- Loan-market activity
- Default expectations

Potential transmission:

Credit deterioration → higher financing costs → reduced investment → earnings pressure → potential equity repricing.

### 4.5 Commodity Channel

Track:

- Crude oil
- Natural gas
- Gold
- Silver
- Copper
- Uranium
- Aluminum
- Critical minerals
- Agricultural commodities

Potential transmission:

Supply disruption → commodity repricing → inflation expectations → central-bank policy expectations → bond and currency repricing.

### 4.6 Risk Appetite Channel

Track:

- Equity volatility
- Credit spreads
- Equity market breadth
- Safe-haven demand
- Cross-asset correlations
- Derivatives positioning
- Volatility term structure

Potential transmission:

Risk aversion → portfolio de-risking → increased demand for selected defensive assets and reduced exposure to vulnerable risk assets.

## 7. REGIME DETECTION

Cross-asset relationships are regime-dependent.

The engine must classify the current macro-financial environment.

### Regime A — Growth Acceleration

Typical characteristics:

- Improving economic activity
- Stronger industrial demand
- Improving earnings expectations
- Potential support for cyclical sectors and selected commodities

### Regime B — Inflationary Pressure

Typical characteristics:

- Rising inflation expectations
- Higher energy or input costs
- Potentially tighter monetary expectations
- Pressure on selected duration-sensitive assets

### Regime C — Disinflationary Expansion

Typical characteristics:

- Moderating inflation
- Resilient growth
- Improving real purchasing power
- Potentially supportive conditions for risk assets

### Regime D — Stagflation Risk

Typical characteristics:

- Weakening growth
- Persistent inflation
- Higher uncertainty
- Conflicting signals across bonds, equities, currencies, and commodities

### Regime E — Liquidity Expansion

Typical characteristics:

- Improving funding conditions
- Greater financial-system liquidity
- Potentially stronger risk appetite
- Support for selected duration-sensitive and risk assets

### Regime F — Liquidity Contraction

Typical characteristics:

- Tighter funding conditions
- Reduced risk capacity
- Potential deleveraging
- Higher sensitivity to leverage and refinancing risk

### Regime G — Financial Stress

Typical characteristics:

- Widening credit spreads
- Funding-market pressure
- Elevated volatility
- Increased demand for liquidity and capital preservation

Regime classification must be evidence-based. Multiple regimes may overlap, and the system must report uncertainty when classification is ambiguous.

## 8. TRANSMISSION SEQUENCE & CAUSAL GRAPH

Every major event must be represented as a directed transmission chain.

Example:

Geopolitical Energy Supply Shock

1. Geopolitical event
2. Physical supply disruption
3. Oil and gas repricing
4. Inflation expectation adjustment
5. Treasury yield repricing
6. Central-bank expectation adjustment
7. Currency-market response
8. Equity-sector repricing
9. Corporate margin implications
10. Portfolio capital reallocation

For every link, record:

- Source evidence
- Expected direction
- Observed market response
- Time lag
- Confidence
- Alternative explanation
- Confirmation status

The engine must distinguish an expected transmission path from one that has actually been observed.

## 9. LEAD-LAG ANALYSIS

The engine must examine the timing of cross-market responses.

Examples:

- Treasury yields may react before equity earnings estimates.
- Commodity futures may react before physical inventory reports.
- Currency markets may reprice before central-bank decisions.
- Credit spreads may reveal deterioration before broad equity indices reflect it.

The system must measure:

- Event timestamp
- First affected market
- Subsequent market reactions
- Time interval between reactions
- Delayed confirmation
- Failed transmission
- Reversal or absorption of the shock

Do not assume that historical lead-lag relationships will remain stable.

## 11. TRANSMISSION IMPACT SCORING

The engine must calculate a Cross-Asset Transmission Impact Score for each significant event.

| Component | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Shock Magnitude | 15% |
| Macro-Economic Relevance | 15% |
| Cross-Asset Exposure | 15% |
| Transmission Evidence | 20% |
| Market Confirmation | 15% |
| Capital-Flow Confirmation | 10% |
| Data Quality & Freshness | 10% |
| **Total** | **100%** |

### Score Classification

- 85–100: Critical Transmission Event
- 70–84: High-Impact Transmission
- 55–69: Moderate Transmission
- 40–54: Limited Transmission
- 0–39: Weak or Unconfirmed Transmission

### Scoring Rules

- Missing data must not be interpreted as confirmation.
- Market reactions must be separated from causal evidence.
- Conflicting cross-asset signals must reduce confidence.
- Scores must include component-level explanations.
- Impact score and confidence must remain separate.
- The score is not a probability of profit.

## 13. SCENARIO & CONTAGION ANALYSIS

Every high-impact event must include:

### Bull Scenario

Conditions under which the event becomes supportive for the relevant asset or sector.

### Base Scenario

The most evidence-supported transmission path under current information.

### Bear Scenario

Conditions under which the event produces a materially adverse market outcome.

### Contagion Assessment

Evaluate whether the shock can spread through:

- Funding markets
- Banking institutions
- Sovereign debt
- Commodity supply chains
- Corporate credit
- Currency markets
- International trade
- Derivatives exposures

For each scenario, specify:

- Trigger
- Transmission path
- Affected assets
- Leading indicators
- Confirmation requirements
- Invalidation conditions

## 14. TRADE & HEDGE OPPORTUNITY GENERATION

The engine may generate conditional investment scenarios only after sufficient evidence is available.

Every opportunity must include:

- Instrument and ticker
- Asset class
- LONG, SHORT, or HEDGE classification
- Macro catalyst
- Transmission thesis
- Market confirmation
- Entry conditions
- Invalidation condition
- Stop-loss, if supported by actual technical data
- Target and risk/reward, if sufficiently grounded
- Expected holding horizon
- Liquidity considerations
- Principal risks

If technical, positioning, or execution data is unavailable, the engine must produce a WATCH scenario rather than inventing an entry, stop, or target.

## 17. OPERATIONAL WORKFLOW

The standard workflow is:

1. Detect a new macroeconomic or geopolitical shock.
2. Register the event and its source evidence.
3. Classify the shock.
4. Identify primary economic variables.
5. Map relevant transmission channels.
6. Identify exposed asset classes.
7. Retrieve and validate current market data.
8. Compare expected and observed market responses.
9. Analyze intermarket relationships.
10. Evaluate lead-lag behavior.
11. Integrate capital-flow evidence.
12. Assess the current macro-financial regime.
13. Build bull, base, and bear scenarios.
14. Calculate the Transmission Impact Score.
15. Identify conditional investment and hedging opportunities.
16. Search for contradictory evidence.
17. Publish relevant findings through GRIOS reporting.

## 19. FINAL OPERATING PRINCIPLE

GRIOS must not analyze markets as isolated instruments.

It must identify the initiating shock, trace the transmission mechanism, measure cross-asset confirmation, monitor capital reallocation, and determine where the economic impact may create an investment opportunity.

The engine must answer seven questions:

1. What happened?
2. Why did it happen?
3. Through which channels will it propagate?
4. Which markets are already reacting?
5. Which markets have not yet confirmed the transmission?
6. Where is capital moving?
7. What evidence would invalidate the investment thesis?
